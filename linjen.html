A voice in a field.

A recording of the line. Forty-six seconds, in Swedish.

Connecting

  1. Det är sällan nån lyfter. It’s rare that anyone lifts.
  2. Härifrån ser jag en väg och sen ingenting. From here I see a road, and then nothing.
  3. Stolparna går åt två håll. Åt ena hållet är det havet, fast man ser det inte. The poles run two ways. One way is the sea, though you can’t see it.
  4. Det stod en kiosk som den här i varje by en gång. Nu är det den här. There was a booth like this in every village once. Now there’s this one.
  5. Jag vet inte vilket år det är hos dig. Det spelar mindre roll än man tror. I don’t know what year it is where you are. It matters less than you’d think.
  6. Strömmen räcker inte längre. Ring igen när det är mörkt. The power won’t hold any longer. Call again when it’s dark.

The line ran out.

Want to talk back? Visit her in Österlen.

Self-funded build. What remains is making it self-sufficient in the field.

How it got here

It began with paper. Stamps. Archive numbers. A kiosk drawn before anyone stood in this field.

The booth arrived by force — cut from concrete, hauled across the county, set where the line would hold.

Then the nights. Voice into silence. Silence into voice. Timing until the answer felt human enough to trust.

The call is open. What remains is to keep it that way — alone, in the field, without help.
